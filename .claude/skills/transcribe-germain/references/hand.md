# Reading the hand

What passes learn about Sophie Germain's handwriting, so that the next one does
not learn it again. **As of 10 September 2026 no pass has run**, so nothing
below is drawn from a view of the papers: it is what is known in general about
French cursive of 1800–1830 and about her idiom from the printed letters, and
it is to be replaced, entry by entry, by what the views actually show. Every
entry added from a pass must name the volume and view it was settled on.

**How to use this file, and how not to.** It narrows a field of candidates for a
stroke you cannot read. It never settles a reading. If the guide says a stroke
is usually *X* and the view in front of you will not support *X*, the view wins
and `\ill{}` stands.

See also `lexicon.md`, generated by `npm run lexicon` from the transcriptions
so far — empty until there are some.

---

## 1. Get the view in front of you properly first

Most "illegible" is under-magnified. Français 9115 is scanned at roughly
5200 × 7100 pixels per view; a whole view on screen shows a line of her hand
a few pixels high. Before deciding a word is lost:

```
npm run tiles -- <volume> <batch>            # six regions per view, from Gallica
npm run tiles -- <volume> <batch> --views 47 # one view
```

The tiles are Gallica's own region crops at full resolution, under
`archives/tiles/<volume>/f<view>/`. `tiles.json` carries the rectangles, so a
tighter region can be asked for by hand with the IIIF region syntax when a word
sits on a seam.

**Turn the leaf in your head, not on disk.** Some views of the correspondence
volumes are from microfilm and may be rotated; a leaf that looks like nothing
may be sideways.

## 2. The spelling of her generation — transcribe, do not flag

| Written | Is | Note |
|---|---|---|
| seroit, avoit, étoit, pourroit | serait, avait, était, pourrait | the *-oit* imperfect and conditional; standard until the 1835 Académie reform |
| connoître, paroître | connaître, paraître | same reform |
| tems, longtems | temps, longtemps | |
| très-grand, très-petit | très grand | hyphen after *très*, usual then |
| enfans, savans, momens | enfants, savants, moments | the *-ans* / *-ens* plurals |
| Mr, Mlle, Mde | Monsieur, Mademoiselle, Madame | as written |
| &, &c | et, etc. | as written |
| c. à d. | c'est-à-dire | |

None of these takes `\uncertain{}` or `\note{sic}`. They are the orthography
of the page.

## 3. Her mathematical idiom — from the printed letters and the 1821 book

To be confirmed against the views.

- **Fermat's equation** as $x^p + y^p = z^p$, and the auxiliary primes as « les
  nombres premiers de la forme $2Np + 1$ », often just « le nombre $\theta$ ».
- **Residues.** « résidus $p^{\text{ièmes}}$ », « résidu » and « non-résidu »
  in Legendre's sense; Gauss's congruence sign in the later manuscripts.
- **Elasticity.** « surface élastique », « lame », « plaque » ; the equation of
  motion with a « somme des courbures » ; « l'épaisseur » of the plate ; « les
  lignes nodales » for Chladni's figures. Partial derivatives with round *d*.
- **Letters** open « Monsieur » and close with a formula; a date at the head
  or foot, sometimes only the day.

## 5. Ambiguous glyphs

Empty. Candidates to watch, from the period rather than from her: the long
*s* in some hands of the 1800s (not expected in hers, born 1776, but check); *u*
against *n* in fast cursive; a final *-s* against a flourish; $p$ against $ρ$
in the residue arguments; 1 against 7 in the tables of auxiliary primes.
