# Reading the hands

What passes learn about the hands in the catalogue, so that the next pass does
not learn it again. The file is organised **by mathematician**, after a section
that holds for every volume. Only the Sophie Germain section has been fed by
passes so far (Français 9115, 9117, 9118, NAF 4073, September 2026); the others
are empty until a pass reads their volumes. Every entry added from a pass must
name the volume and view it was settled on.

**How to use this file, and how not to.** It narrows a field of candidates for a
stroke you cannot read. It never settles a reading. If the guide says a stroke
is usually *X* and the view in front of you will not support *X*, the view wins
and `\ill{}` stands.

See also `lexicon.md`, generated by `npm run lexicon` from the transcriptions
so far.

## Every volume

### Get the view in front of you properly first

Most "illegible" is under-magnified. The BnF's manuscripts are scanned at
roughly 5000 × 7000 pixels per view; a whole view on screen shows a line of a
hand a few pixels high. Before deciding a word is lost, cut the mirror locally:
four full-width bands per view at 2400 px wide read a regular hand end to end;
a tight crop resized to ~1800 px settles most struck and overwritten words. The
mirror is the full IIIF canvas, so this is the same evidence as Gallica's
region crops and costs Gallica nothing. `npm run tiles` fetches those crops
from Gallica when there is no mirror.

**Turn the leaf in your head, not on disk.** Some views are from microfilm and
may be rotated; a leaf that looks like nothing may be sideways.

**Look for the mount.** Leaves are often pasted or hinged onto guards; the edge
of the leaf can pass under the mount and hide the last letters of a line. The
words are not lost, only hidden: say so in a note, and do not complete a word the
sense does not make certain.

### Numbers on the leaves

The library's foliation is usually a thin grey figure at the top right of the
recto, one series across the volume; the writer's pagination is usually in ink,
often at the top left, and often struck through where the leaves were
renumbered. Printers' copy (Français 9117) also carries compositors' names and
« Ch. 4 »-style marks in other hands: record them in notes, as marks, and say
that their meaning is an inference.

### French spelling before 1835 — transcribe, do not flag

| Written | Is | Note |
|---|---|---|
| seroit, avoit, étoit, pourroit | serait, avait, était, pourrait | the *-oit* imperfect and conditional; standard until the 1835 Académie reform |
| connoître, paroître | connaître, paraître | same reform |
| tems, longtems | temps, longtemps | |
| très-grand, très-petit | très grand | hyphen after *très*, usual then |
| enfans, savans, momens | enfants, savants, moments | the *-ans* / *-ens* plurals |
| scavoir, sçavoir | savoir | seventeenth century |
| Mr, Mlle, Mde | Monsieur, Mademoiselle, Madame | as written |
| &, &c | et, etc. | as written |
| c. à d. | c'est-à-dire | |

None of these takes `\uncertain{}` or `\note{sic}`. They are the orthography
of the page.

### Ambiguous glyphs to watch, from the periods rather than from any one hand

The long *s* in hands before about 1800; *u* against *n* in fast cursive; a
final *-s* against a flourish; *p* against ρ in residue arguments; 1 against 7
in tables; Latin contractions (*q̃*, *p̄*, the *-us* hook) in the Latin volumes.

## Sophie Germain (Français 9115, 9117, 9118, NAF 4073)

### Her mathematical idiom

- **Fermat's equation** as $x^p + y^p = z^p$, and the auxiliary primes as « les
  nombres premiers de la forme $2Np + 1$ », often just « le nombre $\theta$ ».
- **Residues.** « résidus $p^{\text{ièmes}}$ », « résidu » and « non-résidu »
  in Legendre's sense; Gauss's congruence sign in the later manuscripts.
- **Elasticity and curvature.** « surface élastique », « lame », « plaque » ;
  « somme des courbures » ; « l'épaisseur » ; « lignes nodales ». In the memoir
  on the curvature of surfaces (Français 9115, v. 43–89): « ligne de distance »,
  « surface des distances », « surface des distances moyennes », « sphère de
  moyenne courbure ».
- **Letters** open « Monsieur » and close with a formula; a date at the head
  or foot, sometimes only the day.

### Strokes that mislead

| The stroke looks like | A pass settled on | Where |
|---|---|---|
| a final « s » on almost every word | a terminal flourish; follow the sense | Français 9115, batch 4 |
| « de la » struck and « sa » kept | her one-word « dela », struck whole | Français 9115, v. 85 |
| a word crossed by a long horizontal | often her joining stroke, not a deletion; zoom before writing `\struck{}` | Français 9115, v. 88 |

### Her fair copy and her drafts

The fair copies are a regular, legible hand; the drafts (Français 9115, v. 93–100)
are fast, overwritten rather than struck, with additions squeezed between the
lines in a much finer pen. Read the fine additions at full resolution from the
mirror; at band scale they are not legible.
